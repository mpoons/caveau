# CellarMentor launch creative: critique, round 2

**Reviewer stance.** I am a creative director from a consumer-app growth team, and I judged only what is visible in the files. The bar is the 12-criterion rubric in `research/02-reference-apps-quality-bar.md` (section 3).
- A touchpoint ships at an average of 4.0 or higher, with no criterion below 3.
- Gates 1, 3 and 10 must each score 4 or higher.

**How I scored gate 10.** The product has 7 private testers and no press, reviews or user base. I therefore scored gate 10 on one question: is anything shown untrue or unproven? A missing testimonial does not lower it. The proof the owner still has to collect is listed separately in section (e).

**What I looked at**
- **nl-nl statics:** all 24 (6 concepts × sq / p45 / st / ls).
- **en-uk:** wanneer-sq, uiteten-p45, nu-sq and erbij-st.
- **nl-be:** wanneer-st, kelder-sq, uiteten-ls and nu-p45.
- **og-nl.png.**
- **Films:** `film-nl-nl-st`, `film-en-uk-sq` and `film-nl-be-p45`, with frames every 1 s, plus finer frames at 0–4 s, 10.5–11 s and 15.8–16.3 s.
- **Landing pages:** `/start/` and `/en/` at 390×844 and 1440×900, each in light and dark, scrolled so lazy images load. I also took viewport shots at the top and bottom of the page to check the sticky CTA.

**Short verdict.** This is a real step up: the overall average went from 3.49 to 3.97.
- The set now has a CTA everywhere, a type-only poster (specifiek), a device-free list (kelder), localised UK UI, consistent demo numbers, honest account microcopy and no real producers on the site.
- Two statics clear the bar outright: **specifiek** and **nu**.
- Three things still separate this from Flighty-level work:
  1. **The label reads as an illustration.** It is the centrepiece of the brand promise and of four touchpoints.
  2. **The film is still a slideshow of UI.** In 9:16 it even hides the scanner's shutter.
  3. **Small sloppiness everywhere:** NIX18 sitting on the phone, rows clipped by the footer, 400 px dead bands in every story, a stray bottle cap peeking over a rule.

     - The label is set in the app's own Playfair italic and EB Garamond.
     - "Saint-Julien" and "APPELLATION SAINT-JULIEN CONTRÔLÉE" run past the edge of the cream panel.

## (d) AI and template tells still present

1. **The headline formula.** Roman statement followed by an *italic bordeaux payoff* appears in wanneer, erbij, uiteten, nu, the hero, the end card and OG. It is fewer than in round 1, but it is still the default move.
2. **One wine everywhere.** The Saint-Julien 2016 is the hero, the scan, the pairing winner, the Horizon row, the list's first row, the OG and Fig. 1. A real cellar has more than one interesting bottle. Its omnipresence signals a single generated asset.
3. **A drawn label standing in for a photo.** For a wine audience this is the clearest tell left. It is synthetic object rendering in the quality bar's own terms: impossible label typography, no glass physics, and a wine that cannot exist.
4. **Zoomed UI at 2–2.5× native.** kelder and erbij enlarge 13–14 px UI text to about 30 px. It looks bloated, and Apple's guidance is to capture native rather than zoom. Inter set big is also the "generic sans" look the rubric warns about.
5. **Floating card or phone with a soft drop shadow** on erbij and wanneer. It is the off-the-shelf promo-template look.
6. **Landing scaffolding.**
   - Zig-zag phone/text sections down the page, a three-column fact strip, two pricing cards and a six-item FAQ accordion.
   - The copy inside is human. The skeleton is every 2025 SaaS page.
7. **Identical vertical rhythm in all st files,** with a dead band top and bottom. It suggests one template scaled up rather than designed for 9:16.
8. **Nothing physical or human.** There is no hand, cellar, glass, plate or person, which is tell #4 ("something is missing"). It is the biggest trust gap for collectors aged 60 and over.

**Clean on the project rules**
- No em dashes in ad or page copy (grep count is 0 on both pages).
- No "niet X, maar Y" constructions.
- No "AI" in any headline or badge.
