# 02: Reference apps and the CellarMentor quality bar

*Research date: 13 October 2026. Every factual claim has a source link next to it. Lines marked **[Analysis]** are my own judgement, not sourced fact. Most growth numbers come from founders or the companies themselves, as reported in interviews or the press. They have not been audited.*

---

## 0. TL;DR

1. **The best launches pair one small product moment with one clear line.** Each of these apps had a single "magic moment" that a 6-second clip or one screenshot can show:
   - Flighty: a delay alert that reaches you before the airline tells you.
   - Cal AI: photo in, calories out.
   - Beli: "which did you like more?"
   - Partiful: the invite itself.

   For CellarMentor the matching moment is **label in, drink window out**.
2. **Craft did the marketing.**
   - Flighty has run no paid social. Its only paid channel is Apple Search Ads, and its main organic driver is a beautifully rendered, shareable Passport ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ryan-jones-flighty-launched-podcast-2025)).
   - Superhuman spent about six months on typography alone ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)).
   - Partiful won Google Play's Best App of 2024 "thanks to its intuitive design" ([Google](https://blog.google/products/google-play/google-play-best-apps-games-2024/)).
3. **Every premium app here launched gated.** Arc, Linear, Superhuman and Beli all used a waitlist or invite-only phase. The gate was used to **filter for the right users and protect retention**, and the scarcity it created was a side effect ([First Round on Linear](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/inside-linear-why-craft-and-focus-still-win-in-product-building/), [First Round on Superhuman](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).
4. **Don't put "AI" at the front of the pitch.**
   - 65% of US consumers want brands to stop "meaningless AI marketing", and 78% find overuse of AI "cringey" ([Harris/4As via Marketing Brew, June 2026](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)).
   - In the Netherlands, 44% disapprove of AI in ads ([Norstat via Emerce, Dec 2025](https://www.emerce.nl/wire/jongeren-kritisch-bijna-helft-vindt-ai-reclames-ok)).
   - McDonald's NL pulled its AI Christmas ad within days of release ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590)).

   Sell the outcome (the right bottle on the right evening) and let the AI stay invisible.
5. **Recommended hero line:** NL "Open elke fles op z'n best." / EN "Open every bottle at its best." Back it with a mechanic-led ad hook ("Scan het etiket. Weet wanneer je 'm opent.") and a positioning line that separates CellarMentor from Vivino ("Een sommelier voor de wijn die je al hebt."). See §4.

## 1. Reference apps (8 analysed)

The selection is weighted toward apps with good evidence. It covers:
- premium and craft apps: Flighty, Arc, Linear, Superhuman;
- fast-growing consumer and subscription apps: Cal AI, Partiful, Beli;
- the wine-category incumbent: Vivino.

Three are food, drink or taste apps: Beli, Vivino and Cal AI.

### 1.1 Flighty: the closest craft analogue to CellarMentor

**What it is.** A premium iOS flight tracker that launched on 23 July 2019 with a 14-day trial, then $8.99/month or $49.99/year at a launch-sale price that later rose to $69.99/year ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2019/07/23/flighty-for-ios-flying-assistant/)). The team built it from January 2018 and beta-tested it with pilots and crew before the August 2019 launch ([Johnny Jet](https://johnnyjet.com/this-essential-flight-tracking-app-that-makes-travelers-better-informed/)).

**Launch narrative.**
- **Positioning:** a "beautiful data-driven flying assistant" that monitors your plane "25-hours before you even depart" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2019/07/23/flighty-for-ios-flying-assistant/)).
- **Coverage:** the first reviews praised design above everything else. MacStories wrote that its "top-notch design alone" put it ahead of rivals ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/flighty-a-pro-level-ios-app-for-frequent-travelers/)).
- **Editors' Choice:** Flighty says Apple named it an Editors' Choice "within days of launch" (summarised in [search coverage of flighty.app/press](https://flighty.app/press)). I could not confirm this independently. The App Store listing does show an Editors' Choice badge today ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flighty-live-flight-tracker/id1358823008)).
- **Awards:** Apple Design Award 2023 and App of the Year finalist ([Flighty press](https://flighty.com/press), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/25/flight-tracker-flighty-adds-a-private-social-network-for-tracking-loved-ones-travels)).

**Landing page today** ([flighty.com](https://flighty.com/)):
- **Hero:** "Get the truth when you travel." That is six words, framed around the outcome.
- **Awards line** immediately under the hero.
- **Product shown as real notification cards** (gate change, delay, landed, baggage belt) rather than as a phone mock-up.
- **Sections follow the journey:** Preflight, At the airport, After landing.
- **Section headers are short and witty:** "See why you're delayed. Finally!", "First to know. First to go.", "Putting the 'Pro' in approachable."
- **Social proof:** press pull quotes ("There are Flight Trackers, and there's Flighty."), named testimonials and "+3 million users".
- **Close:** "Have a nice flight!"

**App Store listing** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flighty-live-flight-tracker/id1358823008)):
- Subtitle: "World's Fastest Delay Alerts."
- First description line: "Don't Fly Without This App."
- Trust line: "The flight tracker your pilot uses."
- Stated principles: no account, no ads, no newsletters.
- Rating: 4.8 from 157K ratings.

**Channels.**
- No paid social or performance UA. Performance marketing was tried three times and "broke even at best".
- Apple Search Ads is the only paid channel and brings "20 or 30%" of new users.
- Ryan Jones: "Every time we chased marketing tactics, we stalled." ([RevenueCat Launched podcast](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ryan-jones-flighty-launched-podcast-2025))
- **Shareable artefacts as growth loops:**
  - The **Passport**, an auto-updating, beautifully rendered flight log with a "black light" effect, is "if it's not our number one growth driver, it's top three".
  - The reworked **Year in Review** "has gone crazy".
  - **Live flight-share links** bring non-users in.

  (All three from [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ryan-jones-flighty-launched-podcast-2025).)
- **Day-one adoption of new Apple features** (Live Activities, Dynamic Island) earned App Store features ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ryan-jones-flighty-launched-podcast-2025), [Apple Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=970ncww4)).

**Why it worked.**
- **A real-world design analogy.** The airport departure board: "one line per flight… they've had 50 years of figuring out what's important." The aim is an app that feels "almost boringly obvious." ([Apple Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=970ncww4))
- **Many concepts per screen.** The team sketches about 20 concepts before settling on one (same source).
- **[Analysis]** The equivalent for CellarMentor is the **sommelier's cellar book and the wine label**. A drink window can be as calm and glanceable as a departure board: one line per bottle, with a status of *Nog wachten / Open nu / Op z'n hoogtepunt / Drink op*. The Passport maps onto a shareable **"Kelderpaspoort" or year-in-wine card**.

### 1.2 Arc (The Browser Company): founder-led storytelling and an invite-only phase

**Launch mechanics.**
- Arc stayed **invite-only for more than a year** and dropped the waitlist at 1.0 on 25 July 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/25/arc-browser-is-now-available-to-download-for-everyone/)).
- To celebrate going public, it opened a **"credits" list** that thanked early adopters by name at arc.net/credits ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/07/25/arc-browser-no-waitlist/)).
- The Windows beta launched to a waitlist of **more than 500,000**, with batched invites, the tweet "The first Arc on Windows beta invites are out – and yours is coming soon," and a playful status site, isarconwindowsyet.com ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/11/arc-browser-launches-its-windows-client-in-beta/)).

**Content style.**
- Weekly Thursday releases with release notes "both personal and professional".
- Engineers filmed feature walkthroughs from their apartments, and CEO Josh Miller posted regular video updates ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/06/20/dia-browser-debuts-with-contextual-ai-chat-but-arc-users-feel-left-behind/)).
- The Feb 2024 "Act II" launch video went viral by:
  - mocking keynote clichés in a self-aware way;
  - restating the problem again and again ("a browser doesn't actually browse");
  - showing everyday use cases and reactions instead of specs;
  - being filmed in a restaurant, not on a stage.

  ([Off-Menu analysis](https://offmenu.substack.com/p/three-reasons-arcs-launch-video-went); video: [x.com/joshm/status/1753129075487478157](https://twitter.com/joshm/status/1753129075487478157))

**Landing page** ([arc.net](https://arc.net/)):
- Uses a single press quote almost as the hero: *"Arc is the Chrome replacement I've been waiting for."* (The Verge).
- Then: "A browser that doesn't just meet your needs — it anticipates them."
- Feature headers are human: "Space for the different sides of you." "Your perfect setup." "The comfort of privacy."
- Four user tweets as social proof.
- Closing CTA: "Enter *your* new home on the internet".

**Caveat.** Arc's novelty limited it to "fanatics" and it was wound down in favour of Dia ([MacMagazine](https://macmagazine.com.br/post/2025/05/28/arc-sera-abandonado-e-empresa-focara-em-novo-navegador-de-ia/), [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/06/20/dia-browser-debuts-with-contextual-ai-chat-but-arc-users-feel-left-behind/)). Its launch craft is a benchmark. Its retention is not.

**[Analysis] What to take for CellarMentor:**
- A short founder-to-camera "kelderbrief" video series.
- A "credits" page of the first 500 founding members (*Fondateurs*).
- A self-aware tone that never mocks the user.

### 1.3 Linear: the benchmark for craft and gated launches

**Launch mechanics** (all from Karri Saarinen on [First Round's podcast](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/inside-linear-why-craft-and-focus-still-win-in-product-building/)):
- **Manifesto first.** Before the waitlist, the team published a blog post about its goals and shared it on X. It "resonated with people who shared the same frustrations".
- **A waitlist of about 10,000 emails**, gated by a **survey** (why do you want this, and what do you use now).
- **Hand-picked cohorts.** Each week Karri picked **5–10 people**, prioritising the most motivated answers. About 90% of invitees signed up and 30–50% became regulars.
- **Launch only with traction.** Linear launched with more than 1,000 DAU.
- **Why:** "we didn't wanna launch it so that we get a lot of people checking it out and then leaving." A waitlist should last "months, not years."
- **Product Hunt came later:** #1 Product of the Day on 30 June 2020 ([hunted.space](https://webmail.hunted.space/product/linear/launches/linear-3)).

**Brand.**
- The [Linear Method](https://linear.app/method) opens: "There is a lost art of building true quality software."
- The homepage ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)) shows real product UI labelled like plates in a book ("Fig 0.1–0.3").
- Every feature section follows the same pattern: short title, one sentence, "Learn more →", visual.
- Proof is three customer quotes plus "over 40,000 product teams".
- **Caveat:** an independent teardown docks the current hero for describing *what it is* rather than *what you get* ([Landing Doctor](https://landingdoctors.com/teardowns/linear-app)).

**[Analysis] What to take:**
- The *"Fig."* captioning convention suits a "Maison/étiquette" editorial feel: *Fig. 1 — Het drinkvenster*.
- The survey-gated waitlist and weekly hand-picked cohorts give CellarMentor quality control during the Dutch beta.

### 1.4 Superhuman: positioning on one attribute, a survey-gated waitlist and white-glove onboarding

**What it did.**
- **One attribute.** Positioned on a single quality, speed: "The fastest email experience ever made" ([Superhuman blog](https://blog.superhuman.com/the-fastest-email-experience-ever-made/)).
- **Price as a signal.** $30/month, chosen at the point where it "starts to feel expensive but isn't out of the question" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)).
- **Typography.** Rahul Vohra tested 15 fonts, rejected them all, and spent six months modifying Adelle Sans ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)).
- **Human onboarding.** Every user got a 1:1 onboarding call, with up to 20 full-time onboarding staff at peak, which created superfans ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)). Survey answers collected at sign-up were used to tailor each call ([Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/5-lessons-i-learned-from-superhumans-onboarding/)).
- **PMF engine.** The Sean Ellis question ("How would you feel if you could no longer use Superhuman?") took the "very disappointed" share from 22% to 58%. The team deliberately limited onboarding and **avoided press** before launch, and used customers' own words for marketing copy ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).
- **Waitlist size.** Reported at more than 250,000 at one point ([FactorDaily podcast](https://podcast.factordaily.com/?p=40027)).

**[Analysis] What to take:**
- Choose one attribute and own it. For CellarMentor that is **timing**: knowing *when*.
- Offer a **"kelder-intake"**, a 20-minute video call where you scan your first 30 bottles together, for the first 100 users.
- Take the hero copy from what beta users actually say.

### 1.5 Beli: a food-taste app (direct taste analogue)

**Launch.**
- Launched on the App Store in June 2021, **invite-only at launch**, with leaderboards and streaks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app))).
- Onboarding required you to **invite four people** before you could sign up, through an auto-generated text ([Demand Curve](https://gp.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-285)).
- A viral Reel of the founder's restaurant map drove thousands of web sign-ups before launch (same source).
- Rolled out city by city with dense local networks, and picked waitlist members on dining activity and location ([8x.social](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/beli-built-cult); a secondary source).

**Mechanic.**
- No 1–5 stars. Users choose between two restaurants they have visited, and Beli derives a personal **Rec Score** and a **Friend Score** ([Demand Curve](https://gp.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-285)).
- "We've gamified ranking" (Judy Thelen, [WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-app-that-makes-rating-restaurants-fun-again-and-gets-better-the-more-you-use-it)).

**Growth.**
- Referrals and word of mouth account for about **80%** of growth.
- Social content (672K Instagram, 404K TikTok) is food-first: the founders narrate off-camera, and the feed is city, cuisine and dish shortlists.
- "We have way more users on the app than followers on social media."
- "Taste is personal" (Eliot Frost).

  (All four from [WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-app-that-makes-rating-restaurants-fun-again-and-gets-better-the-more-you-use-it).)
- **Scale:** more than 75M reviews by September 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app))).
- **Shareable recap:** a mid-2024 "Midyear Snack" recap gave users Instagram-Story infographics ([8x.social](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/beli-built-cult)).

**Landing page** ([beliapp.com](https://beliapp.com/)): a plain hero, "Track and share your favorite restaurants with your friends", then three real screenshots: Track, Share, Discover. **[Analysis]** The landing page is ordinary. The marketing lives in the product loop and on social.

**Caveat.** Some invitees thought the forced invite text was spam ("Were you hacked?") ([Demand Curve](https://gp.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-285)). **[Analysis]** For an older, premium Dutch wine audience, use a softer invite: "Nodig 3 kelder-vrienden uit" should *unlock* something, never *block* sign-up.

### 1.6 Partiful: the invite as the ad

**What it did.**
- Founded in 2020 by Shreya Murthy and Joy Tao ([CNBC via NBC NY](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1)).
- Every invite is a branded touchpoint. Guests can RSVP without an account, see the cover art, and are nudged to install ([NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)).
- In-house designers make "meme-ready" templates such as Charli XCX parody covers and Shrek in sunglasses ([CNBC via NBC NY](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1)).
- Stunt events: the Timothée Chalamet lookalike contest, and a lookalike contest that drew nearly 5,000 RSVPs ([NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)).
- Answered Apple Invites with one wry post on X: "just reviewing the apple developer guidelines" ([NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)).

**Results.**
- Google Play Best App 2024 ([Google](https://blog.google/products/google-play/google-play-best-apps-games-2024/)) and Apple App Store Awards finalist for Cultural Impact.
- About 500K MAU in Q1 2025, up 400% year on year, by Sensor Tower estimate ([CNBC via NBC NY](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1)).

**Landing page** ([partiful.com](https://partiful.com/)):
- Hero: "**Parties are back**" (three words).
- Subhead: "The easiest way to get your guests on the same page."
- CTA: a verb tied to the product, "Create invite", not "Get started".
- Press quotes from NYT Wirecutter and The Atlantic, and "5.0 · 217K ratings".

**[Analysis] What to take:**
- Every shared artefact should carry the brand and work without the app. Examples: a "Fles-uitnodiging" ("we open this 2010 Barolo on Saturday, its window peaks now"), a pairing card, or a cellar-dinner menu card in the étiquette style.
- Pick one wry, human brand voice and keep it the same across channels.

### 1.7 Cal AI: the fast-growing subscription playbook (food-adjacent)

**What it did.**
- A photo-to-calories app. Users snap a meal and get calories and macros ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai)).
- The "magic moment" is easy to show in a short video, so **every creator post doubles as a product demo** (same source).
- About **250 creators on fixed monthly retainers**. The largest earns "tens of thousands per month" for four posts. Creators choose the concept: "They are the professionals. They know what goes viral for their audience." ([Latka interview](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025))
- Roughly half the 17-person team worked on marketing and influencer management (same source).
- The onboarding quiz leads into a Superwall-tested paywall ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/cal-ai-breakdown)).
- 20–25% of new downloads convert to a paid plan or trial in the first session ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)).

**Results (self-reported).**
- Run-rate of more than $35M by May 2025 ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)).
- Acquired by MyFitnessPal: closed December 2025, announced March 2026 ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai)).

**Landing page** ([calai.app](https://www.calai.app/)):
- Hero: "Meet Cal AI / Track your calories / with just a picture." The **mechanic is the headline**.
- Proof in the hero: "Loved by 5M users with ⭐ 4.9 rating".
- An influencer testimonial strip comes straight after the hero.
- Close: "Claim your 3-day free trial."

**App Store** ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616)): the description opens with a **3-step how-to**: answer questions → snap a photo → get your breakdown.

**[Analysis] What to take:**
- The *mechanic-as-headline* pattern for ads: "Scan het etiket. Weet wanneer je 'm opent."
- Fund a curated **wine-creator cohort**: Dutch wine educators, slijterij owners, sommeliers on Instagram and TikTok. Pay them a retainer and give them creative freedom.
- Don't copy Cal AI's hard-sell aesthetic (caps CTAs, star spam). It works for diet apps but contradicts a *Maison* brand.

### 1.8 Vivino: the wine incumbent (what CellarMentor must *not* be)

**History.**
- Started in Copenhagen with an IMDb-for-wine premise ([Windows Central interview](https://windowscentral.com/interview-vivino-ceo-about-their-wine-scanner-windows-phone), [CNN Money 2017](https://money.cnn.com/2017/08/01/smallbusiness/vivino-wine-app/index.html)).
- It took about two years to build a database big enough to be "meaningful" (same sources).
- The label-scan "point the camera" behaviour drove usage: about 6.5 scans per active user per month in 2012 ([Creandum](https://creandum.com/stories/why-we-are-investing-in-vivino)).

**Today** ([vivino.com/uk/app](https://www.vivino.com/uk/app)):
- Hero: "The world's #1 wine app."
- "Join over 70 million people…"
- 3.5B+ scanned labels, 354M+ ratings.
- The homepage hero is about shopping: "Shop top-rated wines" ([vivino.com](https://www.vivino.com/)).

**[Analysis] Implications for CellarMentor:**
- Vivino owns *"scan to rate and buy"* and *"#1"*. CellarMentor can't win on scale, so it should own **the bottles you already have** and **timing** (drink windows), plus in-restaurant wine-list reading.
- **Never lead with the scan alone.** Lead with what the scan *tells you that Vivino doesn't*: *wanneer*, and *waarmee uit je eigen kelder*.

| Paid creators with creative freedom (subscription growth) | Cal AI, about 250 creators on retainer | [Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025) | 10–20 Dutch and Flemish wine creators; brief the *moment*, not the script |

## 3. The CellarMentor quality bar: a 12-criterion rubric

Score every touchpoint (website, ad, App Store or PWA install screenshots, demo video, social post, email) from 1 to 5. **Ship at an average of 4.0 or higher, with no criterion below 3.** Criteria 1, 3 and 10 are **gates**: below 4 means don't ship.

| # | Criterion | 1 = template / AI-slop | 5 = top scale-up standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-sentence clarity** (gate) | The hero needs a subhead to make sense, uses "revolutionize / unlock / elevate", and could belong to any app | A stranger can repeat what CellarMentor does after **3 seconds**. Hero of **≤7 words in Dutch and ≤8 in English**, naming an outcome (Flighty, Partiful) or a mechanic (Cal AI). Subhead ≤20 words with the concrete *how*. No "AI" in the hero ([Harris](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)) |
| 2 | **One owned attribute** | Lists six features with equal weight | Everything ladders up to **timing / "wanneer"**, the way Superhuman ladders to speed ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)). Pairing and wine-list reading appear as *proof of the mentor*, not as rival headlines |
| 3 | **Real product, shown honestly** (gate) | Generated phone mock-ups, fake UI, UI that doesn't exist yet, AI-rendered bottles with garbled labels | **Real captures at native resolution** from the shipping PWA. Apple's rules for previews are a good standard for every ad: "show only content within the app… don't film people interacting with a device… capture the native UI resolution rather than zooming" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)). Every bottle shown is real and photographed |
| 4 | **The magic moment in 6 seconds** | The demo opens on a logo or a lifestyle shot and reaches the product after 10 seconds | The first **3 seconds** show label → drink window, and the core message lands within 3 seconds with the hook in the first 6 ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). The poster frame or thumbnail is the drink-window result ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)) |
| 5 | **Motion craft** | Fast zooms, whip pans, AI morphs, stock "tech" transitions, mismatched easing | **≤30 seconds** in total for previews and ads ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)). Cuts every 1.5–3 seconds, **dissolves and fades only** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)). One easing curve throughout, matching the product's own transitions. One music bed. Real UI sounds (the scan "click", a cork) where useful ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)) |
| 6 | **Sound-off legibility** | The meaning depends on the voiceover. Auto-captions with errors and no house style | Burned-in captions in **EB Garamond small caps or Playfair**, **5–10 words per second at most** ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)), on screen "long enough for people to read" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)). Inside platform safe zones. The ad still makes sense on mute: App Store previews autoplay muted ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)) |
| 7 | **Screenshot-set narrative** | Ten identical "feature + phone" frames; tiny captions; first frame is a splash or login | **Frames 1–3 tell the whole story**, because they are what appears in search when there is no preview ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)). Suggested order: 1) *Scan het etiket* → 2) *Weet wanneer je 'm opent* → 3) *Wat schenk je erbij, uit je eigen kelder*. Then: 4) restaurant wine list, 5) cellar overview, 6) dark mode with the gold accent (Apple asks for one dark-mode shot). Captions of **≤5 words** in Playfair italic on ivory |
| 8 | **Typography and whitespace** | Inter or system sans, centred everything, a gradient behind the headline, three rounded cards ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)) | Playfair Display (italic for wine and producer names), EB Garamond small caps for labels and metadata. **At least 40% negative space** on hero and screenshots. One accent colour per frame (bordeaux #6E1F33 on ivory; gold only in dark mode). A real typographic hierarchy (display / small-caps kicker / body), not three sizes of the same sans. Superhuman-level care: it spent six months on type ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)) |
| 9 | **System consistency** | The site, ads, PWA and social look like four companies | The same "étiquette" grid, label frame, *Fig.*-style captions (as Linear does, [linear.app](https://linear.app/)), colour tokens and voice on every touchpoint. Any frame without a logo is still recognisably CellarMentor |
| 10 | **Truthful, specific social proof** (gate) | "Trusted by thousands", invented stars, stock-photo testimonials, AI-generated faces | Named, real and dated proof placed near the hero, as Flighty, Partiful and Arc do ([flighty.com](https://flighty.com/), [partiful.com](https://partiful.com/), [arc.net](https://arc.net/)). At launch, use only what is true: beta count, named sommelier quotes, press. Never put a number on the page that doesn't exist |
| 11 | **CTA pattern** | "Get started", "Learn more", several competing buttons, caps "CLAIM NOW" | **One verb tied to the product**: Partiful's "Create invite" ([partiful.com](https://partiful.com/)), Flighty's "Give Flighty a try" ([flighty.com](https://flighty.com/)). For CellarMentor: **"Scan je eerste fles"** (primary) and "Bekijk een voorbeeldkelder" (secondary, a demo without sign-up). Repeat the same CTA at the end of the page |
| 12 | **Voice and human specificity** | Contrast framing ("It's not just an app, it's…"), em-dash cadence, triplets, "elevate/unlock/curated" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/the-days-of-the-em-dash-being-a-chatgpt-giveaway-are-over-its-time-to-bring-it-back), [Pencil](https://community.trypencil.com/public/blogs/you-sound-like-a-robot-and-you-didnt-even-use-one-2025-08-15)) | Concrete nouns and real wines: *"Je Château Musar 2012 is nu op z'n hoogtepunt. Tot 2029."* Short, warm, with dry wit in the way of Flighty's "First to know. First to go." ([flighty.com](https://flighty.com/)). Copy is drawn from what beta users say (Superhuman's method, [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)) |

### Additional craft rules (checklist)

**Website**
- Hero order: line → subhead → one CTA → real UI. Proof (one quote or award) goes above the fold.
- **[Analysis]** Section headers of 2–6 words, each with one real UI plate ("Fig. 1").
- Before you go live with any page, build ten alternatives for it and keep the strongest. Flighty sketches about 20 concepts ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=970ncww4)).

**Ads**
- 9:16 and 4:5, at least 720p ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)).
- 15–30 seconds; core message in seconds 0–3; end on a single CTA ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)).
- Two creative lanes:
  - **(a) Maison lane:** brand film made from real UI and real bottles, for site, YouTube and pre-roll.
  - **(b) Creator lane:** a sommelier or wine friend in their own cellar, lo-fi, scanning a real bottle. TikTok itself recommends DIY, creator-style footage ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)), and Cal AI scaled this way ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)).

  The *overlays, captions and end card* stay in house style in both lanes.

**Product UI in ads**
- Real screen recordings only.
- Show touch hotspots only when they clarify an action ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)).
- No transitions that suggest features the app doesn't have ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)).
- No dates or prices that age the asset (same source).

**Disclosure**
- **[Analysis]** If any AI-generated imagery is ever used, label it. The EU AI Act transparency duty is cited in [Norstat/Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/wire/jongeren-kritisch-bijna-helft-vindt-ai-reclames-ok), and a Kantar figure says 59% of Dutch consumers are bothered when AI content is unlabelled (secondary report via [Emerce search coverage](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/nederlander-accepteert-ai-marketing-altijd-menselijke-escape)). The better rule is **no generated imagery in consumer-facing creative at all**.

## 4. Five tells of AI-generated marketing that consumers notice (2025–2026)

1. **A synthetic look in people and objects.** Warped bodies, uncanny expressions, a plastic sheen, morphing shapes.
   - McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas ad (made by TBWA\Neboko and The Sweetshop) about three days after its 6 December 2025 release, after viewers called it "creepy" and "soulless" ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/mcdonalds-pulls-ai-generated-christmas-ad-after-backlash-over-soulless-visuals-and-holiday-chaos)).
   - Valentino's morphing-handbag AI video was criticised as "cheap and unsettling" for a craft brand ([McGraw Hill summary of BBC/LA Times](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/blog/2026/05/ai-slop-backfires-in-ads.html)).
   - **[Analysis] Wine-specific version:** AI-rendered bottles with gibberish labels, impossible glass reflections, a "sunset vineyard swirl" stock look. Wine people read labels closely and will catch it at once.
2. **The generic landing-page template.** Inter typeface, an indigo-to-purple gradient behind a centred hero, three rounded cards with thin-line icons, "Build faster. Ship smarter.", "Get Started" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). The model returns "the median" of its training examples (same sources).
3. **Copy rhythm.** Constant "it's X, not Y" contrast framing, em-dash add-on clauses, three-beat lists, a too-neat summary line, and words like "elevate", "tapestry", "meticulous", "nuanced" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/the-days-of-the-em-dash-being-a-chatgpt-giveaway-are-over-its-time-to-bring-it-back), [Pencil community](https://community.trypencil.com/public/blogs/you-sound-like-a-robot-and-you-didnt-even-use-one-2025-08-15)). TechRadar notes that individual tells are unreliable, but readers react to the pattern.
4. **"Something is missing."**
   - Seven in ten consumers say AI-generated ads feel like they are "missing something".
   - 78% prefer human-made ads.
   - 87% say top advertising still needs a person involved.

   (Canva State of Marketing & AI 2026, n=3,547 across 7 countries, [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/canva-ai-marketing-consumer-trust-gap-report).) **[Analysis]** In practice, "missing" means no specific person, place, product detail or point of view.
5. **Leading with "AI" itself.**
   - 73% would trust an ad less if they suspected AI made it.
   - 63% would be less likely to buy.
   - 78% find brands that overuse AI "cringey".
   - 65% want brands to stop "meaningless AI marketing".

   ([Harris Poll/4As/Infillion via Marketing Brew, June 2026](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions).) Only 21% trust ads made entirely by AI, though 73% accept AI-assisted ads that feel relevant ([Smartly, Sept 2025](https://www.smartly.io/resources/ai-and-advertising-today-what-consumers-expect)).

   **Dutch data:**
   - 44% of Dutch adults disapprove of AI in ads, websites and flyers, rising to 53% among those 60 and over, which is CellarMentor's core collector age ([Norstat, n=1,020, Oct 2025, via Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/wire/jongeren-kritisch-bijna-helft-vindt-ai-reclames-ok)).
   - Only 33% of Dutch people are willing to trust AI, against 46% globally ([KPMG/Univ. of Melbourne via Consultancy.nl](https://www.consultancy.nl/nieuws/60444/nederlanders-hebben-minder-vertrouwen-in-ai-dan-rest-van-de-wereld)).

**Counter-evidence.** Coca-Cola's 2024 AI holiday ads reportedly tested well with general audiences ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/coca-cola-releases-second-ai-holiday-ad-ai-becomes-norm)). A System1/Jellyfish test reportedly found no emotional penalty for ads that were recognisably AI (secondary report in [search coverage](https://martech.org/consumers-want-ai-ads-with-a-human-touch/)). So the risk is concentrated in *visible* AI and in craft or premium categories, which is where CellarMentor sits.

**[Analysis] Implication.** "AI" should appear in CellarMentor marketing only as a *how* in the body copy ("herkent het etiket"), never in the hero, logo lock-up or ad headline. Copy such as "Vraag de sommelier" is fine. "AI-powered" is out.

## 5. Positioning one-liners (NL + EN)

The context is Vivino owning *scan / rate / buy / #1* ([vivino.com/uk/app](https://www.vivino.com/uk/app)). CellarMentor's open ground is **bottles you already own** plus **timing**, extended to the restaurant wine list.

### Option A: an outcome line (the Flighty or Partiful pattern), recommended as the hero

- **NL:** *Open elke fles op z'n best.*
- **EN:** *Open every bottle at its best.*
- **Why:**
  - Six words in both languages.
  - It names the emotional outcome: never again opening a great bottle too early or too late.
  - It holds the owned attribute, *timing*, without saying "drink window" (jargon) or "AI" (a trust penalty).
  - It works the way "Get the truth when you travel" ([flighty.com](https://flighty.com/)) and "Parties are back" ([partiful.com](https://partiful.com/)) do: a feeling first, with the subhead explaining the mechanism.
  - "Op z'n best" is natural spoken Dutch, and *op z'n hoogtepunt* can become the in-app status.
- **Suggested subhead (NL):** *Scan het etiket. CellarMentor vertelt je wanneer je 'm opent, wat je erbij schenkt uit je eigen kelder, en leest de wijnkaart voor je mee.*

### Option B: a mechanic line (the Cal AI pattern), recommended for ads, App Store frame 1 and the PWA install prompt

- **NL:** *Scan het etiket. Weet wanneer je 'm opent.*
- **EN:** *Scan the label. Know when to open it.*
- **Why:**
  - Cal AI's hero "Track your calories with just a picture" makes the action the promise, and every creator video became a demo of it ([calai.app](https://www.calai.app/), [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/cal-ai)).
  - This line *is* the 6-second storyboard: label → window.
  - Its "know when" is what separates it from Vivino's scan, which ends in a rating or a shop link.
  - **Risk:** it puts the scan, where Vivino is strong, up front. That is why it works better as an ad hook than as the brand line.

### Option C: a category-reframe line (the Superhuman and Arc pattern), recommended for PR, About and investor framing

- **NL:** *Een sommelier voor de wijn die je al hebt.*
- **EN:** *A sommelier for the wine you already own.*
- **Why:**
  - Superhuman named a new category on one attribute ("the fastest email experience ever made", [Superhuman blog](https://blog.superhuman.com/the-fastest-email-experience-ever-made/)).
  - Arc used a frame of "your new home on the internet" ([arc.net](https://arc.net/)).
  - This line cleanly separates CellarMentor from Vivino, which is a sommelier for wine you might *buy*.
  - It explains the "Mentor" in the name.
  - It suits the Maison register.
  - **Weakness:** it is less immediately concrete than A or B, so pair it with a real UI plate.

**[Analysis] Recommendation.**
- **Hero:** A.
- **Paid and social hook, screenshot 1, creator brief:** B.
- **Manifesto, press release and the "Over ons" page:** C.

Before locking the copy, run a five-second test with 20 Dutch wine-cellar owners: show the hero, hide it, and ask them to say what the app does. That measures criterion 1. Then rewrite using their own words, as Superhuman did ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).

**Lines to avoid:**
- "De slimste wijnapp" or "AI-powered wine assistant": AI-first, and covered by the Harris finding.
- "Je wijnkelder, opnieuw uitgevonden": a template line.
- "Not just a cellar app, it's…": contrast framing, an AI tell.
- "#1": you can't claim it, and Vivino owns it.

## 6. Launch-channel blueprint derived from the references [Analysis, with source patterns]

1. **Manifesto post plus a survey-gated waitlist**, as Linear did ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/inside-linear-why-craft-and-focus-still-win-in-product-building/)).
   - Survey: bottle count, regions, "wat ging er mis met je laatste dure fles?"
   - Admit 25–50 people a week, hand-picked.
   - Aim for months, not years.
2. **White-glove "kelder-intake" calls** for the first 100 users, as Superhuman did ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/superhumans-secret-to-success-rahul-vohra)). Ask the Sean Ellis question after two weeks of use.
3. **Shareable artefacts built in from day one**, as Flighty and Beli did ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ryan-jones-flighty-launched-podcast-2025), [8x.social](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/beli-built-cult)):
   - a Kelderpaspoort;
   - an "Open deze maand" card;
   - a dinner-invite card in étiquette style;
   - a year-in-wine recap in December.
4. **Founding-member credits page**, as Arc did ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/07/25/arc-browser-no-waitlist/)): "Cuvée 1 — de eerste 500".
5. **Creator cohort on retainer**, as Cal AI did ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)). 10–20 Dutch and Flemish wine voices, each with a brief built around one moment.
6. **Founder video cadence**, as Arc did ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/06/20/dia-browser-debuts-with-contextual-ai-chat-but-arc-users-feel-left-behind/)). A monthly "kelderbrief": one wine and its window, filmed in a real cellar.
7. **Product Hunt only after retention is proven**, as Linear did ([hunted.space](https://webmail.hunted.space/product/linear/launches/linear-3)). Dutch press (wine magazines, tech) comes before Product Hunt, given the Dutch-first audience.
