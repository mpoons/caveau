# 03 · Alcohol-advertising rules for CellarMentor paid media (NL/BE → UK/US)

*Research date: 13 October 2026. This is regulatory desk research, not legal advice. Every rule below cites the official page I read. Where a page shows a "last updated" date I give it; "no date shown" means the page itself carries none. Where I could not read the official page directly (JS-only or blocked), I say so and mark the point **UNVERIFIED**.*

---

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **CellarMentor is not an alcohol seller, but its ads will usually be treated as alcohol ads** once they show wine bottles, labels, glasses or drinking. That is how the platforms work: Meta covers "ads that promote **or reference** alcohol", including "depictions of alcohol brands or logos". Google covers ads that "focus on or raise awareness of alcoholic beverages". TikTok covers ads that "feature branding of alcoholic products". Snap covers ads that "promote or reference alcohol". The UK CAP Code s.18 explicitly covers marketing that "feature[s] or refer[s] to alcoholic drinks". Dutch and Belgian self-regulation turn on whether the ad (in)directly promotes alcohol, judged by content and how the public perceives it. **Plan every creative that shows wine as if it were an alcohol ad.** Ads that show only the app UI and food, with no recognisable wine, can usually stay outside scope.
2. **Two hard "no's" in our launch markets:**
   - **TikTok in NL and BE.** The Dutch RVA, art. 23 lid 7, bans alcohol ads on TikTok outright. The Belgian Convenant, art. 13 as applied by the JEP, bans them on TikTok and Snapchat. The Netherlands is also absent from TikTok's own alcohol market list.
   - **Snapchat in BE.**
3. **Norway is out for any wine imagery.** The Alkoholloven bans letting alcohol feature in the marketing of *other* goods and services. Meta, Snap, Google and Pinterest all prohibit alcohol ads there too.
4. **A conservative default that satisfies every regime we checked:**
   - Age-target **25+ in NL/BE/UK** (legal floor 18) and **25+ in the US** (Meta/Snap floor 21; TikTok US requires 25).
   - Never show anyone who is or looks under 25.
   - No drinking-in-progress shots.
   - No health, social or sexual-success claims.
   - No "drink it now" urgency.
   - Add **NIX18** (NL) and the **Belgian slogan banner** (BE) to any creative where wine is prominent.
   - Keep real third-party wine labels out of ads.

## 1. Does an ad for a wine *app* count as alcohol advertising?

| Regime | Scope test (quoted/paraphrased) | Applies to CellarMentor ads showing wine? |

| Google Ads / YouTube | "Branding and informational ads: These ads focus on or raise awareness of alcoholic beverages" are in scope (alongside sales ads). | **Probably yes** when wine is the visual or copy focus. Automated review may label the ad "Alcohol" and limit it to permitted countries ("Eligible (limited)"). |
| TikTok | Applies to anyone who wants to "advertise, promote, sell, or feature branding of alcoholic products and beverages". | **Yes** if labels or brands are visible. |

## 2. Platform policies

### 2.1 Meta (Facebook / Instagram)

Source: [Meta Ad Standards › Restricted goods & services › Alcohol](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol). Change log: latest dated entry **26 Jun 2024**.

- **Classification.** Alcohol is a sub-policy of "Restricted goods and services". There is no separate "alcohol vs restricted goods" track. Ads referencing alcohol must comply with "all applicable local laws, required or established industry codes, guidelines, licenses and approvals", plus country and age targeting. For NL that means the RVA, for BE the Convenant and for the UK the CAP Code.
- **Age.** The minimum is 18 everywhere. Higher minimums:
  - US 21
  - Canada 19
  - Japan 20
  - Iceland 20
  - Korea 19
  - **Sweden 25**
  - India varies by state
  - **NL, BE and UK have no higher Meta minimum, so 18+ applies.**
- **Prohibited countries** include **Norway** and **Lithuania**, plus Russia, Turkey, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Thailand and others.
- **Creative.** Must not breach Community Standards. Educational or PSA content is allowed. Non-alcohol products are fine if they "don't depict alcohol or its consumption".
- **Practical note.** The policy itself gives no worked examples. Meta enforces largely by automated classification, so wine-bottle creatives without the correct age floor tend to get rejected. *Ambiguity:* Meta does not say whether an informational wine app is an "alcohol ad". Assume yes once wine is visible.

### 2.2 Google Ads: Search, Display, YouTube, Performance Max, Demand Gen

Source: [Google Ads policy › Alcohol](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6012382?hl=en). **No date shown** (©2026).

- **Scope.** Sales ads, **"branding and informational ads"**, mixers, accessories, and 0.0% drinks (0.0% only certified in IN/ID/EG).
- **Countries.** You may target only the listed countries. NL, BE, UK and US are listed. **Norway is not.** Mixed-country campaigns run only in the listed ones ("Eligible (limited)").
- **Age.** "Must not target people under the legal purchase age". No numeric table is given. YouTube offers age-gating for channel and video.
- **Creative.**
  - No misstating strength or emphasising high ABV.
  - No encouraging excess.
  - No claims of improved physical, mental or sporting ability, social or sexual success, removed inhibitions, or health or therapeutic value.
  - No drinking before or during skilled activities.
  - Nothing that primarily appeals to minors. Minors are never shown drinking. No pregnant people drinking.
- **Disallowed formats (non-exhaustive).**
  - App install image and video ads (irrelevant for a PWA)
  - Consumer ratings annotations
  - **Discovery ads**
  - Dynamic Search Ads
  - Gmail ads
  - Reservation display and Google TV masthead
- **Ambiguity: Demand Gen and Performance Max.** Discovery ads were replaced by **Demand Gen**, but the policy page still says "Discovery". It does not mention Demand Gen or PMax. Expect alcohol-classified creatives to be limited or not served on Discover and Gmail inventory inside Demand Gen and PMax. **UNVERIFIED: test with one campaign before scaling.**
- **Certification.** **No alcohol certification is required for NL/BE/UK/US.** Certification exists only for Poland (informational beer) and 0.0% in IN/ID/EG.
- **Landing page.** The ABV-display rule applies to drinks sold or promoted. It is not relevant unless CellarMentor pages are judged to "promote" specific wines.

### 2.4 Snapchat

Source: [Snap Advertising Policies § 3.2 Alcohol](https://snap.com/en-US/ad-policies). **No date shown.**

- **Scope.** Ads that "promote or reference alcohol".
- **Age.** 18+ or local drinking age: Canada 19, JP/TH 20, **US 21**, **Sweden 25**, India 18/21/25.
- **Banned countries** include **Norway** and **Lithuania**, plus Algeria, Bahrain, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Morocco, Nigeria, Oman, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Tunisia, Turkey and UAE.
- **Creative.**
  - No appeal to under-age people.
  - No excess, and no negative portrayal of abstinence.
  - No performance, health or success claims.
  - No driving or skill activities.
  - **No one drinking who appears under 25.**
- **Disclaimer.** A mandatory warning such as **"please drink responsibly"** or the local equivalent must be "prominently displayed".
- **Belgium.** The Convenant/JEP treats Snapchat as lacking adequate age-gating, so alcohol ads there are banned (§3.4).

## 3. Law and self-regulation by country

### 3.1 Netherlands: statutory law

- **Alcoholwet, art. 2.** Allows the government to set rules by AMvB on the content, audience, time, manner and place of alcohol advertising.
  - **Not used so far.** Confirmed as of the Kamerbrief of 21 Oct 2024 ([Kamerstuk 32 793 nr. 784](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2024D39857), staatssecretaris Karremans).
  - That letter set out options (a total ban, a partial ban, or more self-regulation) but adopted no measure.
  - **UNVERIFIED for 2025–26:** I found no adopted national online alcohol-ad law.
  - The pending Alcoholwet amendment **36884** (submitted 19 Jan 2026) concerns online-sales age checks and other matters, not advertising ([Tweede Kamer bill page](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/wetsvoorstellen/detail?qry=wetsvoorstel%3A36884&cfg=wetsvoorsteldetails)).
  - The Alcoholwet's other rules (age-18 sales, price-promotion limits) bind sellers, not CellarMentor.
- **Mediawet 2008.** Alcohol ads on linear TV and radio only between 21:00 and 06:00. There are no Mediawet alcohol rules for on-demand video or video-uploaders, per the same Kamerbrief.
  - **Influencers (video-uploaders):** since **16 June 2025** the CvdM dropped its 500k-follower threshold.
  - Uploaders are now covered if they are KvK-registered, have posted 24 or more videos on YouTube, Instagram or TikTok in 12 months, and earn money or products. They must make advertising recognisable and protect minors ([NOS, 28 May 2025](https://nos.nl/l/2569106)).
  - **Ambiguity:** [Ondernemersplein](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/reclameregels-voor-social-media-influencer-marketing/) still lists ">100,000 followers". Treat it as outdated. The CvdM site returned HTTP 502 when I tried to confirm.
- **ACM (consumer law).** [Leidraad Bescherming Online Consument (May 2024)](https://www.acm.nl/system/files/documents/leidraad-bescherming-online-consument-2024.pdf):
  - Any compensation (money, free product, discount, affiliate) makes a post advertising.
  - **The advertiser is co-responsible** and must check its influencers.
  - A mere @-tag is not sufficient disclosure.

### 3.2 Netherlands: Reclamecode voor Alcoholhoudende Dranken (RVA, STIVA / Stichting Reclame Code)

Source: [reclamecode.nl › RVA](https://www.reclamecode.nl/nrc/reclamecode-voor-alcoholhoudende-dranken-rva/). **Last amended 1 April 2024**; transition for art. 29 (sport) and art. 32 (NIX18) ended 1 Oct 2024. Enforced by the Reclame Code Commissie (RCC).

**Scope and applicability to a non-producer.** The code applies to "alle op het Nederlandse publiek gerichte reclame" for alcohol. Reclame is "iedere ... directe dan wel indirecte aanprijzing van alcoholhoudende drank" (above 0.5% ABV). RCC case law decides by content and public perception:
- [Lay's "PERFECT WITH BEER", 2020/00201](https://www.reclamecode.nl/uitspraken/gezondheid/voeding-en-drank-2020-00201/274570/): **RVA not applicable**. The packaging "is duidelijk bedoeld om de daarin verpakte chips aan te prijzen". The beer reference "speelt hierbij een ondergeschikte rol".
- [Wijnvoordeel shirt sponsorship, CvB 2017/00873](https://www.reclamecode.nl/uitspraak/?uitspraakId=208936): **RVA applied** to a wine webshop's name on a sports shirt. Intent is irrelevant; what matters is how the public reads it.
- **Read-across for CellarMentor (my assessment, ambiguous):** an ad that sells *the app*, with wine visually subordinate, is closer to Lay's. A creative where a recognisable bottle or label or a pour is the hero edges towards "indirect promotion". **Comply voluntarily** on wine-prominent creatives. The cost is small: 25+ models and a NIX18 logo.

**Key rules (article numbers per the current text):**

| Art. | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | No excessive or irresponsible consumption shown, suggested or encouraged. The toelichting names emptying a glass in one go, and glasses of 2× a standard serving or more (wine standard = 100 ml). |
| 2 | Abstinence or moderation must not be shown negatively. |
| 6 | No references to possible health benefits. No claims of improved physical, mental or sporting performance. |
| 8 | No causal link to social or sexual success. |
| 9 | Not aimed at pregnant women. |
| 10 | Not specifically aimed at minors (youth language, idols, toys). |
| **11** | No persons under 18 or "evident jonger lijken dan 18". **For staged ads with hired models, or in collaborations: no one under 25 or evidently looking under 25.** Paid (well-known) persons, incl. influencers, must be 25+ and look it. |
| 13 / 17 | No risky, violent or aggressive behaviour. No link with traffic participation. |
| **20** | Alcohol advertising "in welke vorm dan ook" must not reach an audience **more than 25% minors**. The burden of proof is on the advertiser. |
| 22 | No alcohol ads on youth channels, magazines or websites. |
| **23** (social media) | Applies on top of the NRC, RSM and Mediawet. **Lid 2:** online image ads must show the **NIX18** logo clearly. **Lid 3c:** use the **18+ age filter where the platform offers one**, for both organic and paid content (YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and X are named). **Lid 3d–e:** where there is no filter, verify that commenters are 18+, or post a clear 18+-only notice. **Lid 4:** for selectable recipients (e-mail, cookie or profile targeting), use a minimum age of 18 as an effective criterion. **Lid 5:** before posting on or replying to third-party accounts, verify the owner is 18+. **Lid 6:** distributors and influencers must be **25+ and look it**, and label posts **#Ad / #advertentie** per the RSM. **Lid 7:** **"Het is niet toegestaan om reclame voor alcoholhoudende dranken te maken op het platform TikTok"**, including via influencers. The ban lapses once TikTok has a working age filter. **Lid 8:** cooperate with opt-out for adults. |
| 24 | Websites with an alcohol brand in the domain need an age check (at least birth year). Not applicable to cellarmentor.com. |
| **32** | **"Iedere reclame voor alcoholhoudende drank dient het logo 'NIX18' te tonen in het beeld."** It may be combined with **"Geniet, maar drink met mate"**. The slogan is optional and the logo is mandatory. Online commercials: at least 0.75% of the frame and at least 10 s, or the full duration if shorter. Banners and internet marketing: at least 1.25% of the frame (banners of 120×60 px or smaller are exempt). The logo must be horizontal and isolated, with clear contrast. Influencers may use "NIX18" or "#NIX18" in the caption instead. |

### 3.3 Netherlands: Reclamecode Social Media & Influencer Marketing (RSM)

Source: the RSM text published on reclamecode.nl, labelled **"UPDATE 2026"** (exact effective date not captured; **verify**).

- **Who is covered.** A "Verspreider" is anyone with a "Relevante Relatie" with the advertiser: payment, free product or loan, invitations, discount codes, affiliate links, reposts or giveaways at the advertiser's request, or commissions. **No follower minimum**: nano-influencers and **UGC creators** are covered.
- **Disclosure labels.** "AD/ad", "Reclame", "Advertentie", "Advertorial", "(Betaald) partnerschap", "(Betaalde) promotie", "(Betaalde) samenwerking", or the platform's paid-partnership feature, combined with tagging the advertiser.
  - "Affiliate" or "commissie" alone is **not** enough.
  - A label only in the bio is not enough.
- **Placement.**
  - Photos and posts: the label is the **first word or sentence** of each text block.
  - Video: the label appears at the start, during the promotion, or as a persistent overlay, **and** first in the description.
  - Stories: every frame is labelled.
  - Livestreams: repeat the label regularly.
- **Alcohol note in the RSM toelichting.** Where ads may not target minors, as with alcohol, the platform age filter must be used. This often requires the platform's paid-partnership feature.
- **Other rules.**
  - The advertiser has a duty of care (*zorgplicht*, RSM art. 6, also referenced by RVA art. 23 lid 3b).
  - No fake likes or followers.
  - AI-generated or virtual-influencer content must not mislead.

### 3.4 Belgium

**(a) Self-/co-regulation: the Convenant inzake reclame voor en marketing van alcoholhoudende dranken (enforced by the JEP)**

Sources:
- Current text: [PDF, version **22 April 2026**](https://raadvoorreclame.be/app/uploads/2024/10/Conv-reclame-en-markt-alcohol-v.220426.pdf). It replaces the 9 Oct 2024 version, which applied from 1 Jan 2025.
- [Raad voor Reclame announcement (27 Nov 2024)](https://raadvoorreclame.be/strengere-regels-voor-alcoholreclame/)
- [JEP/RvR Q&A webinar (27 Nov 2024)](https://raadvoorreclame.be/qa-webinar-alcoholconvenant/)

**The "Arnoldus" name:** the convention is a successor of the 2005 convention, which was updated in 2013 and 2019. I did not find "Arnoldus" used in the current official texts.

**Scope and applicability:**
- **Art. 1.1:** advertising is "elke mededeling die rechtstreeks of onrechtstreeks ten doel heeft de verkoop van alcoholhoudende dranken te bevorderen". Alcohol means above 0.5%.
- **Q&A:** the convention applies to alcohol advertising by **all advertisers**, signatories or not. Whether something is alcohol advertising "depends on how it looks and what it says".
- **Q&A on jurisdiction:** for online communications, the **advertiser's country of origin determines the JEP's territorial competence**. As a Dutch B.V., a Belgian complaint would likely be routed to the Dutch SRC via EASA cross-border handling. **UNVERIFIED** how the SRC would apply Belgian rules.

**Key rules:**

| Art. | Rule |
|---|---|
| 3 | No irresponsible or excessive consumption. No psychological or physical benefits, stimulant or calming effects, or performance gains. No social or sexual success. No denigrating abstinence. Alcohol not presented as necessary for happiness or a festive mood. |
| **4.2** | Must not depict persons **younger than 25 or appearing so**. |
| 4.5 | No characters or techniques popular with minors, incl. influencers and youth culture. |
| 6 | **No doctors, medical professionals, medical clothing or settings.** No health improvement. Not aimed at pregnant women. No disease claims. |
| 7–9 | No link with driving, good work performance or sports performance. |
| 10 | ABV may be mentioned only as information. High ABV is never a quality. |
| 12.2 | No alcohol ads in digital media (websites, apps, social) aimed specifically at minors. That means **an audience of at least 30% minors**, or a youth-appealing overall impression where data is lacking. |
| 12.4 / 12.5 | Use platform techniques to stop minors seeing alcohol ads, and to stop users sharing them with minors. |
| **13** | Alcohol ads are **banned on social platforms without adequate age-gating**. The Q&A names **TikTok and Snapchat**: accounts were to be removed or made invisible in BE from 1 Jan 2025. Instagram is acceptable because Meta offers age-gating. |
| 14 | Influencers sponsored by alcohol brands use the age filters and must be **25+ and look it**. |
| **Annex B.5** (digital) | Every digital ad carries the educational slogan **"Alcoholmisbruik schaadt de gezondheid"** / **"L'abus d'alcool nuit à la santé"**. English or German versions are allowed if the audience requires. The slogan sits **at the bottom, full width, on a contrasting background band**, in **Open Sans**. Minimum size is **32 px at 1080×1080**, other formats proportional (at least 2.5 mm), with padding above and below equal to the text height. On video it shows for the whole spot. It must not be integrated into the photo. For an Instagram Reel the band must stay visible, and the JEP suggests repeating the slogan in the caption. |
| 16 | Sanctions: €50 lump sum for a missing or incorrect slogan, €1,000 per day for non-compliance with a decision, and mandatory pre-clearance for 12 or more months for repeat offenders. |

**(b) Upcoming statutory rule (Royal Decree), not yet in force**

Sources: [VAD, 21 Apr 2026](https://vad.be/artikels/wetgeving-voor-alcoholmarketing-wordt-strenger/); [VRT NWS, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/26/belgische-brouwers-kanten-zich-tegen-slogan-alcohol-schaadt-de/). The minister's own press page ([vandenbroucke.belgium.be](https://vandenbroucke.belgium.be/nl/nieuws/sterkere-bescherming-van-minderjarigen-bij-nieuwe-regels-alcoholreclame)) returned HTTP 403 to me.

- **Status.** The Council of Ministers approved it on **27 Mar 2026**. As of 26 Aug 2026 it still had to complete an EU procedure (presumably the TRIS notification). It takes effect **one year after publication** in the Belgisch Staatsblad. **Publication not confirmed as of 13 Oct 2026.**
- **Content as reported.**
  - Mandatory warning **"Alcohol schaadt de gezondheid"** in a contrasting colour, covering **at least 5% of the ad surface**. Labels and packaging are exempt.
  - Ban on advertising in media aimed mainly at minors (**30% or more of the audience minor**), incl. digital and streaming.
  - Ban on influencers with a predominantly minor following.
  - Ban on free alcohol giveaways.
  - Enforced by FOD Volksgezondheid.
- **For us.** Design the BE template so the band can be swapped from the current convention slogan to the KB slogan at the larger size.

### 3.5 United Kingdom: CAP Code section 18 (non-broadcast), ASA

Source: [CAP Code s.18 Alcohol](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/18.html). **No date shown** on the code section.

- **Scope.** Covers ads for alcoholic drinks (above 0.5%) and marketing communications that "feature or refer to alcoholic drinks". **This applies to CellarMentor ads showing wine.**
- **18.1** No encouraging unwise or excessive drinking.
- **18.2** No implying that alcohol boosts confidence or popularity. Watch copy like "impress your guests".
- **18.10** Promotions must not encourage excess.
- **18.12** No link with unsafe activities or driving.
- **18.14** Nothing likely to appeal particularly to under-18s, incl. youth culture.
- **18.15** "No medium should be used to advertise alcoholic drinks if more than **25%** of its audience is under 18".
- **18.16** People shown drinking or playing a significant role "must neither be nor seem to be **under 25**". Under-25s may appear only if obviously not drinking, for example at a family celebration.
- **18.17** No health, fitness or weight-control claims.
- **Online targeting guidance.** [CAP Advertising Guidance: Age-restricted ads online](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/advertising-guidance-age-restricted-ads-online.html), plus [ASA advice: Alcohol – targeting & appeal to under-18s](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/alcohol-the-young.html).
  - Age targeting alone is often not enough. The ASA expects **age targeting plus interest-based targeting**, and audience evidence.
  - Rulings have been upheld where advertisers could not show their under-18 audience share (e.g. an AU Vodka TikTok ad in 2025, per ASA reporting; I did not read the ruling itself).
- **Not applicable to us:** the Portman Group Code, which binds alcohol producers.

### 3.6 United States (brief)

- **TTB / FAA Act** advertising rules bind producers, importers and wholesalers ([27 U.S.C. §205](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/27/205)). **Not CellarMentor.**
- **The FTC Act §5 and the Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)** apply to everyone: truthful claims, and clear disclosure of paid creators.
- **Industry codes** are voluntary and not binding on us, but the platforms reference them.
  - The Wine Institute Code requires models depicted as wine consumers to be **25+**.
  - Wine ads run only where at least **73.8%** of the audience is 21+ (2020-census update) ([Wine Institute ad code](https://wineinstitute.org/our-work/responsibility/social/ad-code/)).
- **Platform floors:** Meta, Snap and Reddit (per extracts) require 21+. TikTok US requires **25+** and a Sales Rep.

### 3.7 Norway (and Nordics)

- **Norway.** Alkoholloven §9-2, per the [Helsedirektoratet commentary](https://www.helsedirektoratet.no/rundskriv/alkoholloven/kapittel-9-reklameforbud/-9-2.reklame-for-alkoholholdig-drikk), prohibits:
  - all alcohol advertising,
  - advertising for goods bearing alcohol brand marks, and
  - **letting alcohol feature in marketing of other goods or services**.

  Whether a given image counts is a case-by-case purpose test. A wine-cellar app's ads would very likely fail it. Meta, Snap, Google and Pinterest also block alcohol ads in Norway. → **Norway: UI-only creatives, or skip it.**
- **Sweden.** Meta, Snap and TikTok all require **25+** targeting. **Finland:** Pinterest prohibits alcohol ads; TikTok has extra limits. **Lithuania:** a total ban.

### 3.8 EU overlay

- The AVMSD (as amended by Directive 2018/1808) sets the baseline for alcohol audiovisual commercial communications: no minors, no performance, social or sexual success, therapeutic or high-ABV claims. The NL and BE codes implement it.
- The **DSA (art. 28)** separately bars platforms from showing profiling-based ads to users they know are minors. This helps, but it is not a substitute for our own age settings.

### 4.2 Creative guidelines that keep ads safe on every platform and in every regime

**People**
1. **Everyone on camera is 25+ and visibly so.** Keep age documentation (ID copy or model release) for every model and creator. This satisfies RVA art. 11 and 23(6), BE art. 4.2 and 14, CAP 18.16, TikTok, Snap and the Wine Institute code. *No exceptions for "friends at the dinner table" shots.*
2. **Prefer hands, tables and phones to faces.** A hand holding a phone over a cellar rack is the safest hero shot.
3. **No pregnant people, no doctors or medical clothing, no sports kit or athletes, no cars or driving, no work or office settings with wine** (BE art. 6–9; RVA art. 6, 9, 17, 29; CAP 18.12).

**Wine imagery**

4. **Lead with the app.** UI, a label-scan animation and drink-window charts should dominate. Bottles are context. This is the "Lay's test": the product promoted is the app.
5. **No drinking-in-progress.** No sips, no gulps, no clinking toasts as the climax. If a glass appears, it is a normal serving (wine standard is about 100 ml; never a glass of twice that or more) and stands on the table.
6. **No real third-party wine brands or labels in paid ads.** Use fictional or own-designed labels, or blur them. This avoids "depicting alcohol brands" (Meta), trademark and endorsement issues, and the "brand campaign" categories on TikTok and in Norway.
7. **Never emphasise ABV, price discounts on wine, "bargain" bottles or free wine** (BE art. 10; RVA art. 4 and 19; TikTok prize/discount ban).

**Copy**

8. **No health or functional claims:** "relaxing", "good for the heart", "unwind" or "sleep" are all out. **No social or sexual success:** avoid "impress your guests" and "be the wine expert everyone admires". Phrase benefits around **knowledge and organisation**: "know what's in your cellar", "open each bottle at its best".
9. **No consumption urgency.** Avoid "drink it before it's too late", "time to open more bottles" and "never waste a bottle again—drink now". Prefer "your 2019 Rioja is in its window" or "plan which bottle to open for Sunday's dinner".
10. **Don't denigrate abstaining or moderation.** No "dry January is for quitters" humour.
11. **No youth culture:** memes, slang, cartoon characters, gaming or teen music trends (RVA art. 10; BE art. 4.5; CAP 18.14).

**Labels, disclaimers and landing page**

12. **NL:** put the **NIX18 logo** on every creative where wine is visible. It is cheap insurance under RVA art. 32. Influencer posts can use "#NIX18" instead.
13. **BE:** add the **full-width bottom band** reading "Alcoholmisbruik schaadt de gezondheid" (NL) or "L'abus d'alcool nuit à la santé" (FR), in Open Sans, ≥32 px at 1080×1080, visible for the whole video. Keep the template ready to switch to "Alcohol schaadt de gezondheid" at ≥5% of the surface once the Royal Decree is in force.
14. **TikTok, Snap, Microsoft (and Reddit if on the alcohol path):** add a responsible-drinking line, e.g. "Geniet met mate · 18+" (NL) or "Please enjoy responsibly · 21+" (US).
15. **Landing page (cellarmentor.com):** add a visible "18+ only" statement (21+ for US visitors) and an age confirmation at sign-up. TikTok and Microsoft require age gating for alcohol ads, and RVA art. 23(3) asks for an 18+ notice where no filter exists. Avoid linking ads to pages that list specific wines with prices, to stay out of "sales" categories.

**Targeting and placement**

16. **Default age floor 25+ in NL/BE/UK and 25+ in the US.** The legal or platform minimums are 18 / 18 / 18 / 21 (TikTok US 25). A cellar app's real buyers are 30+, so the reach loss is small.
17. **Layer interest targeting** (wine, gastronomy, cooking, travel) on top of age, as the ASA expects. **Exclude** gaming, school or student, youth-music and kids' content.
18. **Keep audience-composition evidence.** Screenshot the platform age breakdowns per campaign: under 25% minors (NL, UK) and under 30% (BE).
19. **Geo-exclude** Norway, Lithuania, Sweden (unless the floor is 25+), Finland on Pinterest, and Gulf states for any campaign containing B/C creatives.
20. **Run organic social with the same rules.** The RVA and BE convention cover organic posts on your own channels too. Set Instagram and Facebook account age restrictions (18+ NL/BE, 21+ US) if wine content is posted.

**Influencers and UGC (NL/BE)**

21. Creators must be **25+ and look it** (RVA art. 23(6); BE art. 14).
22. Label every post as **"Advertentie"/"#ad" first**, plus the platform's paid-partnership tool. The tool is also needed for age-filtering.
23. Add **#NIX18** and, for Belgian audiences, the slogan.
24. **No TikTok creator deals for NL or BE.**
25. Contractually require age-gated posts. Remember that **UGC creators count as "Verspreiders"** under the 2026 RSM, and that creators who are KvK-registered, post 24 or more videos a year and are monetised fall under the **Mediawet / CvdM**.
